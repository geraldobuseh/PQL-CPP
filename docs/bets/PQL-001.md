# PQL-001: v0.1 Bet Register

A reproducible system for comparing real investments, systematic paper strategies, and SPY can improve investment decision-making and reveal whether any strategy demonstrates persistent edge.

---

## Hypothesis

**Core Claim**: 
A deterministic, auditable system that tracks real investment positions, paper-trades systematic strategies, and benchmarks both against SPY (total return) can reveal whether any investment approach demonstrates persistent edge—defined as excess risk-adjusted return beyond the passive benchmark after accounting for all costs.

**Falsifiable Prediction**:
If the system is built correctly and run for sufficient periods, strategies with genuine edge will consistently outperform SPY (after fees, transaction costs, and drawdown), while strategies without edge will not.

---

## Problem

**What we don't know:**
- Can systematic rules for selecting investments outperform passive index investment?
- Are my current investment choices repeatable and measurable?
- What fraction of my real returns came from skill vs. market conditions vs. luck?
- How do paper strategies perform relative to real execution constraints (slippage, timing)?

**Why it matters:**
Without a system to compare strategies fairly, it's impossible to distinguish luck from edge. A strategy that makes money in a bull market may fail in a flat or down market. Positive returns alone prove nothing.

---

## Learning Objective

Understand whether:
1. Real portfolio outperforms SPY after fees
2. Any paper strategy outperforms SPY after simulated costs
3. Outperformance persists across market regimes (bull, flat, bear)
4. Outperformance is due to repeatable rules or timing luck
5. Any strategy survives extreme drawdown with acceptable volatility

---

## Generation Target

Build a system that:
- Records all real investment transactions with dates, prices, fees
- Simulates paper strategies deterministically with identical price data
- Computes portfolio NAV for both real and paper strategies
- Calculates returns and risk metrics (volatility, max drawdown, Sharpe ratio)
- Compares strategy performance to SPY total return over the same period
- Tracks costs (trading fees, slippage estimates, opportunity costs)

---

## Resolution Signal

**The bet is RESOLVED POSITIVE** if:

1. A strategy (real or paper) outperforms SPY total return over a **multi-year period** by:
   - Excess return ≥ 3% annualized (after all fees and costs), AND
   - Sharpe ratio ≥ SPY Sharpe ratio, AND
   - Maximum drawdown ≤ 1.5 × SPY maximum drawdown for the same period, AND

2. Outperformance is repeatable in a **second, independent period** under similar market conditions

3. The outperformance is attributed to **systematic rules**, not isolated timing luck

**The bet is RESOLVED NEGATIVE** if:

1. After tracking real positions and paper strategies for 2+ years:
   - All strategies underperform SPY after fees, OR
   - All strategies that outperform do so only during bull markets, OR
   - Outperformance reverses in the subsequent period, OR
   - Excess return is insufficient (< 3% annualized)

2. Or if the implementation reveals that:
   - Transaction costs are larger than expected, making edge impossible
   - Real execution (slippage, timing) differs materially from paper assumptions

**Explicit Rejection**:
- ✗ "Strategy made money" does NOT resolve this bet positively
- ✗ "Strategy beat SPY for one year" does NOT resolve this bet positively
- ✗ "Strategy had positive returns" does NOT resolve this bet positively
- ✗ Comparison to anything other than SPY total return is out of scope

---

## Constraints

- All calculations must be deterministic: same inputs → same output
- Real transactions are immutable; no revision of historical trade records
- Strategies must use only information available at decision time (no look-ahead bias)
- Simulations use same price data as real portfolio (no data-snooping optimism)
- Fees, slippage, and taxes are estimated conservatively (never optimistically)
- SPY is the only benchmark for v0.1; multi-index comparison is out of scope

## Appendix: Benchmark Definition

**SPY (SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust)**
- Tracks S&P 500 Index
- Total return (includes dividends reinvested)
- Fees: ~0.03% annually
- Liquidity: Highly liquid; minimal slippage on entry/exit
- Accessibility: Available to retail investors

**Why SPY?**
- Reflects broad market exposure available to any investor
- No skill required; passive, diversified, low-cost
- Any strategy must beat this passive baseline to claim edge
- SPY data is historical, verifiable, not subject to selection bias

**Comparison method:**
- Calculate SPY total return for exact period: start of first transaction → present
- Calculate strategy return (real or paper) for same period
- Compute excess return: strategy return − SPY return
- Adjust for fees and costs within strategy calculation
- Compare risk metrics (Sharpe, max drawdown) for full period

## Appendix: What Edge Actually Means

**Edge is NOT:**
- Making money (bull markets make money)
- Beating SPY for one year (luck can explain this)
- High returns (high-risk bets can achieve high returns)
- Outperforming without costs (costs are real)

**Edge IS:**
- Excess risk-adjusted return persistently across multiple periods
- Repeatable by following consistent rules
- Surviving implementation costs, taxes, and timing slippage
- Unexplainable by luck or market conditions alone

A strategy with genuine edge would:
- ✓ Outperform SPY after fees in bull markets
- ✓ Outperform SPY (or lose less) in bear markets
- ✓ Repeat performance in an independent period
- ✓ Have a documented, repeatable decision rule
- ✓ Work without requiring perfect timing or perfect fills
