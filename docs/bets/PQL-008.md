# PQL-008: Immutable transaction records

## Bet and resolution signal

An immutable record containing attribution, fill facts and fees can become the sole
financial event source for portfolio replay. Resolve the bet by rebuilding the
ticket example and fee-bearing variants exactly, while preserving atomic rejection
and the non-negotiable strategy snapshot boundary.

The learning checkpoint is `notes/004-ledgers-and-event-sourcing.md`, including why
storing financial events is safer than only retaining a mutable balance.

## Transaction contract

`Transaction::create(PortfolioId, const Trade&, Money fees)` returns optional Transaction.
Fees must be finite and nonnegative; Money provides finiteness and the factory adds
the sign check. All inputs are strongly typed. Portfolio ID, order ID, symbol, side,
quantity, execution price, fees and timestamp have read-only accessors. The owned
Trade already enforces valid positive quantity/price, side and execution time.

The record can be copy-constructed, including from rvalues, but not assigned or
edited. There are no public aggregate/default constructors or setters. Copying does
not modify the source. Identity remains `(PortfolioId, OrderId)` for the current
one-full-fill-per-order model; partial fills need a separate future identity design.

## One authoritative history path

Portfolio and PortfolioSnapshot now hold `vector<Transaction>`, replacing the earlier
Trade-only history. `applyTransaction()` validates portfolio attribution before any
financial update, then preserves chronology, deduplication, cash/position validation
and the candidate-copy/no-throw commit. `replay()` accepts only Transaction history
and invokes that same path. No parallel Trade history is retained.

The PQL-007 `applyTrade()` remains a documented zero-fee adapter: it creates a
Transaction attributed to the receiving portfolio and delegates. Callers with fees
must create an explicit Transaction. Existing zero-fee accounting tests retain their
numeric expectations; only their history representation and replay inputs migrate.

Position remains symbol-level accounting. Its fee-aware `with_trade(Trade, Money)`
overload accepts fees from the Transaction; its original overload explicitly uses
zero fees. It rejects negative fees independently. Portfolio attribution belongs
to Portfolio, not Position. Snapshot has neither transaction nor trade application.

## Fee and value policy

Buy cash debit is `quantity * price + fees`. Those fees enter weighted-average basis.
Sell cash change is `quantity * price - fees`, and realized increment is
`quantity * (price - average cost) - fees`. A partial sale keeps remaining average cost.

Net sale proceeds may be zero or negative. Zero still appends history and updates
shares; a negative change is permitted only if existing cash covers it. Fees can
exactly cancel gross realized profit. These cancellations are valid zeros, not
arithmetic failures. Overflow or a wholly swallowed contribution is rejected.

With zero fees, the ticket history starting at $1,000 reconstructs $920 cash, one
AAPL share, $100 remaining basis and $20 realized P&L. At an explicit $120 mark,
market value is $120, unrealized P&L $20 and total value $1,040.

With buy fee $2 and sell fee $1, the same fills produce $917 cash, one share at
$101 average, $18 realized, $19 unrealized and $1,037 total value at that mark.

## Scope and validation

Tests cover record immutability/ownership, typed fields, explicit fees, both oracles,
exact replay/live equivalence, weighted purchases, partial/full sales, wrong portfolio,
duplicate identity, negative fees, fee-driven insufficient funds, zero/negative net
sale proceeds, cancellation and numerical failures. Existing chronology/oversell/
mark checks remain in the regression suite.

History is an in-memory source of financial facts plus separately retained starting
cash. This ticket introduces no database migration, external cashflow, correction,
partial-fill, live broker or strategy framework. Supplied marks and their freshness
remain outside the ledger; past execution prices are not silently reused as current
marks. Approximate arithmetic and its rejection policy remain explicit.
